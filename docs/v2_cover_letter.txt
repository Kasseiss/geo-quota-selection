Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript “Geographic allocation and visual coverage for data efficient land cover segmentation” for publication in Remote Sensing.

The study examines how source-region allocation complements visual coverage when a small number of training images must support land cover segmentation in other regions. It compares actual and size-preserving permuted source groups within shared selection rules, evaluates source-mean weighting within facility location, and follows the quota comparisons through an available-supervision-pixel intervention.

The main matrix contains 576 fits evaluated on a fixed 24-region panel. Geographic quota k-center improves on visual k-center by 1.68 mIoU percentage points in the complete-model analysis and by 2.00 points when available processed supervision pixels are matched. Geographic weighted facility location improves on ordinary coverage, while quota random provides a strong simple reference. Pixel matching reduces the actual-versus-permuted quota contrast, showing that the allocation and membership comparisons respond differently to the supervision budget.

The manuscript provides evidence for allocating limited training resources across geographic sources. Supplementary materials report the complete conditions, source-specific effects and experimental execution sequence. The accompanying research folder preserves the processed data provenance, selected image identities, code, confusion matrices and numerical results.
